A running list of blog posts that I've found interesting, impactful, or just worth remembering. This list is small right now — I wish I'd been keeping track all along — but it'll grow.

---

**[The Three Paragons of IT: Chidi, Ted & Jules](https://tombridge.com/2021/07/08/the-three-paragons-of-it-chidi-ted-jules/)** — Tom Bridge

A MacAdmins Conference talk framing three pop culture characters — Chidi Anagonye, Ted Lasso, and Jules Winnfield — as archetypes for IT virtues. It's a clever and genuinely useful lens for thinking about what good IT work looks like. The slides are worth a read even if you never watch the video.

---

**[End of Year IT Reflections](https://tombridge.com/2025/12/01/end-of-year-it-reflections/)** — Tom Bridge

A custom set of 20 end-of-year reflection questions tailored specifically for IT professionals. Less navel-gazing, more practically useful than most year-in-review frameworks.

---

**[This Is Not the Computer for You](https://samhenri.gold/blog/20260312-this-is-not-the-computer-for-you/)** — Sam Henri Gold

A piece that took me right back to late-night childhood tinkering sessions — pushing a machine past its limits, crashing it beyond recovery, then reinstalling from the recovery disks, relieved the machine was alive again. A reminder that humble beginnings — breaking things and putting them back together — can lead to a career, a passion, or at minimum the confidence to keep going.

---

**[You have more time than you think. Here are 5 science-backed ways to find it.](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/488838/time-management-calendar-busy-productivity)** — Vox / Future Perfect

Five concrete, research-backed strategies for reclaiming time: track how you actually spend it, run a weekly planning session, engineer a better workday, identify your golden hours, and pair big dreams with small plans. Less productivity-guru fluff than most pieces on the topic. *(Full article requires a [Vox](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/488838/time-management-calendar-busy-productivity) membership or [Apple News+](https://apple.news/AqNmmkB4uRkWGaQRLyGB8Kw) subscription.)*

---

**[Can We Preserve Knowledge… Forever?](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/24/nx-s1-5797776/can-we-preserve-knowledge-forever)** — NPR *(TED Radio Hour)*

A TED Radio Hour episode on what it actually means to preserve human knowledge in the digital age — and how fragile the systems we've built to do it really are. A significant portion focuses on the Internet Archive, which makes the question feel both urgent and quietly hopeful.

---

**[Can You Rewire Your Brain?](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/emily-mcdonald-can-you-rewire-your-brain/id1710609544?i=1000750535423)** — *What Now? with Trevor Noah*

Emily McDonald on neuroplasticity — what the science actually supports, what "rewiring your brain" means in practice, and where the hopeful self-help mythology diverges from reality.

---

**[Conditional Values Aren't Values](https://kitzy.com/blog/conditional-values-arent-values/)** — Kitzy

On what it actually means to hold a value — and how values that only apply when it's convenient aren't really values at all. A short read with a sharp point.
